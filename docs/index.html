PeerCheck: interactive demo

Two models label a UAV image sequence of a footbridge from a few annotated keyframes: SAM 3 propagates the keyframe masks along the sequence, and a light decoder on frozen DINOv2 features segments each frame on its own. Where their labels agree, the frame is accepted with the consensus label; where they disagree, it goes to a person.

What to try

Play the clip and switch layers. Around frames 155 to 164 the pier comes into view: the SAM 3 labels propagated from the keyframes miss it, while the DINOv2 decoder finds it. The two models disagree, Ci falls, and these frames go to review; the consensus marks the disputed pixels as ignore. Lower τ to about 0.6 to see the risk: the same frames would then be accepted with inadequate labels.

Manual time

Annotating a frame takes 157 s and checking an automatic label 7 s, as measured in the study. A reviewed frame whose label falls below q is annotated again. This clip was chosen to show both outcomes and is harder than the dataset as a whole; over all 659 frames the effort at 5% keyframes is 18% to 23% of annotating every frame.

About the data

The clip is frames 141 to 168 of one image sequence, between two keyframes of the paper's main run (5% temporal uniform keyframes, first run), with predictions from that run. Images are shown at reduced resolution; scores are those of the paper at full resolution. The ground truth is used only for evaluation.
